After-hours emergency call routing for HVAC with GHL

At 9:15 p.m. in July, “missed call” is not a CRM metric — it is a no-cool homeowner deciding which shop gets the morning slot. HighLevel can own the first digital response and on-call ping; Jobber still owns the truck. This page is the routing playbook between those two systems.

Safety & compliance: Gas smell, CO alarm, electrical sparking, or medical distress → instruct callers to use emergency services where appropriate. Register A2P / brand for SMS, honor STOP, and get consent where required. This is not legal advice. Carrier filtering punishes spammy after-hours copy.

What “good routing” means for HVAC nights

True after-hours emergency

  • No heat in freezing weather / no cool for medically vulnerable
  • Active leak at the air handler or condensate overflow risking damage
  • System dead after a storm with food-safety or occupancy risk

Goal: human on-call notified fast; SMS acknowledges; Jobber job created once triage is clear.

Urgent but bookable

  • AC weak, strange noise, thermostat confusion
  • Filter / maintenance requests that can wait until open
  • Estimate-only shopping calls

Goal: text-back + booking link or next-morning CSR queue — do not wake a tech for a filter.

Write that split in one sentence your owner, CSR, and on-call tech sign. If the policy only lives in someone’s head, GHL workflows will disagree with the truck by week two.

Recommended night path (GHL + Jobber)

  1. Write a one-page after-hours policy

    Include: office hours, who is on-call this week, what counts as roll-tonight vs. first-thing, after-hours fees (if any), and the SMS promise you will actually keep (“callback within 15 minutes” only if you mean it). Tape it next to the phone and paste it into the GHL workflow notes.

  2. Map every public number into a clean path

    Inventory Google Business Profile, LSA, website click-to-call, truck magnets, and yard signs. Marketing and after-hours overflow should forward into a HighLevel number (or ring group) that can fire missed-call workflows. Known-customer DIDs can still hit the office/on-call cell if you prefer — document the diagram so nobody “fixes” forwarding at 2 a.m. Phone/SMS usage fees apply; see HighLevel pricing and usage docs.

  3. Define urgency keywords and queues

    In the first SMS, offer clear replies: URGENT, YES (callback), booking link, STOP. Branch the workflow:

    • URGENT → notify on-call immediately (GHL app + SMS to manager cell) + create opportunity in an “After-Hours Emergency” pipeline stage.
    • YES / book → CSR morning queue or calendar for next open diagnostic; tag after-hours-non-emergency.
    • No reply → one softer follow-up next morning only (avoid harassing overnight).

  4. Build missed-call SMS + on-call notify in HighLevel

    Reuse the skeleton from the text-back setup guide, then harden it for nights:

    • Trigger on missed call / voicemail on the after-hours path.
    • Wait 30–90 seconds to avoid double-texting redialers.
    • Skip if do-not-sms or a text went out in the last 10 minutes.
    • Send a short contractor-sounding SMS (template below).
    • Notify on-call; escalate to backup after N minutes with no acknowledgment if your team can support it.

    Example after-hours SMS (edit hard):

    Hi — {{contact.company_name}} missed your call after hours. Reply URGENT for no-heat/no-cool emergencies (we’ll alert on-call). Reply YES for a morning callback, or book: {{booking_link}}. For gas smell or CO alarm, call emergency services. Reply STOP to opt out.

    Never promise “tech in 20 minutes” unless winter staffing makes that true. Name the trade. Keep segments short to control SMS cost.

  5. Hand booked emergencies into Jobber

    Ops stays in Jobber: on-call or morning CSR confirms address, system type, access, and fee expectations, then creates the request/job and assigns the truck there. If you use the official Jobber↔HighLevel connection, contact fields can sync — jobs, notes, and attachments do not (sync details). Tag the GHL contact booked / rolled-tonight so nurture stops.

  6. Run a seven-night QA drill

    • Miss a call to the ads number from a personal cell on at least three nights.
    • Reply URGENT once and YES once — confirm the right human is notified.
    • Verify STOP, booking link on iOS/Android, and no duplicate contacts.
    • Log: missed → text → reply → Jobber job. If URGENT never reaches a phone that is awake, the workflow is theater.

When to keep a live answering service in the mix

GHL text-back is excellent at speed and written record. It is weaker when the caller will not text, needs voice reassurance, or your insurance/dispatch culture demands a live agent. Common hybrid:

  • Answering service answers live for true voice triage on the main after-hours line.
  • HighLevel owns SMS on missed marketing calls, voicemail drops, daytime overflow, and the CRM trail into Jobber.

Do not cancel answering on day one of a GHL trial in peak season. Model both on the comparison page and the calculator — outputs are estimates only. All-in software cost context for a mid-size shop: 5-truck GHL pricing.

Plumbing & electrical notes

Plumbing: route burst / slab leak / sewer backup like HVAC URGENT; clogged sink usually books morning. Electrical: never coach DIY on live panels in SMS; push sparking / burning smell to emergency services + on-call. Same GHL skeleton; change keywords and calendars.

FAQ

Should on-call live inside the HighLevel mobile app?

They need notifications that wake them — GHL app, SMS to cell, or both. Building jobs still happens in Jobber. Do not ask techs to run the whole night out of a marketing CRM.

How many follow-up texts after midnight?

Prefer one acknowledgment overnight. Pile-on SMS feels like spam, burns usage budget, and hurts deliverability. Continue in the morning if they have not booked.

Where does calendar booking fit?

Great for non-emergency diagnostics and morning first-available. For roll-tonight emergencies, a human confirm beats an open self-serve calendar that overbooks a single on-call tech.
