GHL vs answering service: cost & recovery math

Home-service owners usually ask the wrong binary: “answering service or software?” The useful question is which mix covers after-hours, overflow, and speed-to-lead without paying twice for the same job.

What each option is actually good at

Answering service

  • Live voice — calm a panicked homeowner
  • Can follow a scripted triage (gas smell, no heat, breaker tripped)
  • Monthly fee often scales with minutes / calls
  • Does not own your SMS nurture, review ask, or ad attribution
  • Quality varies wildly by vendor and night shift

HighLevel (missed-call + SMS)

  • Instant text when a call is missed — high open rates
  • Booking link + pipeline + on-call notify in one system
  • List pricing from $97 or $297/mo + usage SMS/phone
  • Pairs with Jobber for ops (sync notes)
  • Not a substitute for judgment on true emergencies

Break-even framing (GHL alone)

At a $450 ticket, covering a $97 plan + ~$30 SMS mid-band (~$127) takes well under one average job per month. That does not mean the software “pays for itself automatically” — you still need the workflow live and someone answering the replies. See methodology for formulas.

Decision guide

  • Lean GHL-first if most losses are unanswered marketing calls and voicemail black holes, and your team will monitor SMS.
  • Keep answering if you sell high-urgency work and callers refuse to text, or insurance/dispatch rules demand a live voice.
  • Hybrid if winter after-hours volume is brutal — do not cancel answering on day one of a GHL trial.
